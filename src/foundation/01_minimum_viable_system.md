# Minimum Viable System

Version: 0.1.0  
Evidence profile: A–D depending on the component

## Purpose

Build a daily system simple enough to follow even on low-motivation days.

## Key takeaway

A plan fails when it requires perfect days. The minimum viable system protects progress on imperfect days.

## The six anchors

### 1. Sleep anchor

Choose one wake-up time and keep it within a 60-minute window most days.

**Minimum:** protect at least 7.5 hours in bed.  
**Target:** roughly 8 hours for most nights.  
**Rule:** caffeine should not be used to compensate for chronically insufficient sleep.

### 2. Food anchor

Every main meal contains:

- one clear protein source,
- vegetables or fruit,
- a controlled carbohydrate portion when useful,
- and a measured source of fat rather than free-pouring oil.

**Minimum:** three protein-containing meals.  
**Target:** meet the daily protein range established in the nutrition module.

### 3. Movement anchor

**Minimum:** 20 minutes of walking.  
**Target:** 8,000–10,000 steps plus the programmed workout.

A missed workout becomes a walk, not a zero-activity day.

### 4. Training anchor

**Minimum:** complete the first two compound movements.  
**Target:** complete the full session.

This rule prevents the “all or nothing” pattern.

### 5. Recovery anchor

Spend five minutes on slow breathing, mobility or quiet decompression.

A simple option:

- inhale through the nose for 4 seconds,
- exhale slowly for 6 seconds,
- repeat for 5 minutes without forcing the breath.

### 6. Review anchor

Before sleep, record:

- sleep plan,
- training completed,
- steps,
- tobacco status,
- one sentence about energy and mood.

## Decision rules

| Situation | Response |
|---|---|
| Slept poorly | Reduce training intensity; keep the walk and normal meal structure |
| Missed breakfast | Do not “punish” yourself; continue with the next planned meal |
| Craving tobacco | Delay 10 minutes, drink water, walk, use a planned quit aid if prescribed |
| Missed workout | Perform the minimum session or 20-minute walk |
| Ate more than planned | Return to the next normal meal; do not starve the next day |
| Motivation is low | Follow minimums only; protect the chain |

## Day template

### Morning

- Wake within the selected window.
- Drink water according to thirst; no need to force excessive amounts.
- Get daylight exposure when possible.
- Eat a protein-containing breakfast if breakfast suits the schedule.
- Review the day’s one essential task.

### Midday

- Eat a structured meal.
- Take a short walk.
- Complete the workout when scheduled.

### Evening

- Eat the final planned meal without turning it into unrestricted snacking.
- Stop caffeine early enough that it does not disturb sleep.
- Prepare clothes, food and equipment for the next day.
- Reduce bright screens before bed.

## Common mistakes

1. Changing the entire life in one day.
2. Setting an aggressive calorie deficit.
3. Training hard every day.
4. Treating one poor meal as a failed week.
5. Using supplements before fixing sleep, food and training.
6. Measuring progress only through body weight.

## Checklist

- [ ] Wake time selected
- [ ] Sleep window protected
- [ ] Three protein-containing meals planned
- [ ] Walking minimum defined
- [ ] Training minimum defined
- [ ] Five-minute recovery practice selected
- [ ] Evening review ready

## When professional help is appropriate

Seek professional support when there is persistent chest pain, fainting, severe shortness of breath, suspected eating disorder, repeated inability to stop tobacco despite attempts, severe depression or anxiety, or persistent sexual-function problems.
